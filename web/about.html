ABOUT GAVI

Cameras see.
GAVI understands.

What General Artificial Visual Intelligence actually is, why it's not "just call a vision model on every frame," and how it works under the hood.

THE GAVI PROJECT · 2026 · 8 MIN READ

What GAVI is

GAVI is a layer of general intelligence that you attach to any video feed — a CCTV camera, a webcam, a drone, a doorbell. Point a feed at it and it starts doing what a good human observer does: it notices who and what is in the scene, gives each of them a stable identity, keeps a timestamped log of everything that happens, and reasons about the parts it didn't directly see.

The one-line version: GAVI gives cameras a memory. Everything else follows from that.

The value proposition, in three questions

Every vision product can answer "what is in this frame?" GAVI is built for the questions that only make sense across time:

  • "Who is this?" — not a person, but e3, the person who arrived 40 minutes ago, left for 10, and came back. Identity that survives exits, re-entries, and restarts.
  • "What happened while I wasn't looking?" — he left with the bag and returned without it; GAVI infers he set it down off-screen, and records that as an inference, clearly separated from what it actually saw.
  • "Has anyone…?" — been loitering for 20 minutes without buying, left a bag unattended for an hour, entered after hours? Plain-English rules, evaluated against the whole timeline, not a single frame.
Frame-by-frame AI answers "what is this?" GAVI answers "what is going on?"

"Isn't this just calling Gemini on every frame?"

Fair question — GAVI does use a vision-language model as its eyes. But a raw API call is stateless: every frame starts from zero. Ask a VLM about frame 401 and it has no idea what it said about frame 400. In practice that means:

  • The same person is a new person in every frame — no identity, no dwell time, no history.
  • Nothing exists once it leaves the frame. There is no "still tracked," no "returned after 3 hours."
  • There is nothing to ask questions of. No timeline, no memory — just a pile of disconnected captions.

And the naive fix — pasting the model's previous answers back into its prompt — fails in a subtler way. When we built GAVI's first prototype that way, we measured the model anchoring on its own memory: it kept "seeing" objects for several frames after they left, and kept missing them after they returned, because the prompt told it what to expect. Perception biased by memory is worse than no memory.

GAVI's answer is an architecture, not a bigger prompt:

  1. Perception stays memory-blind. The VLM describes each burst of frames with zero context, so it can only report what's actually in the pixels.
  2. Identity is decided by code, not by the model. A deterministic matcher fuses appearance signatures with motion continuity — things don't teleport — and solves the assignment optimally. Two people in identical outfits stay two people.
  3. Memory lives in a world model the language model can consult but never overwrite: a registry of entities and an append-only event log, where entries/exits are derived by code — so they cannot be hallucinated.
Honesty is a feature. When two identical-looking people genuinely can't be told apart, GAVI flags the match as identity_uncertain instead of silently guessing — and its inferences carry confidence levels, kept clearly separate from observations. A monitoring system you can't trust about its own uncertainty is just a liability with a dashboard.

Where we're pointing it first

Anywhere someone currently pays a human to stare at screens — or simply accepts not knowing. The common thread: the value is in watching over time, not in any single frame.

01 · RETAIL

Loss prevention & loitering

Dwell time per person, wandering patterns, "browsing for 25 minutes, bought nothing, keeps returning to the same shelf."

02 · CONSTRUCTION

Site safety & PPE

Who's on site, who's missing a hard hat, which zones are active — narrated and alerted from existing cameras.

03 · FACILITIES

Multi-camera security

Nine feeds, one brain: unattended bags, after-hours presence, people down — with a timeline you can interrogate afterwards.

04 · LOGISTICS

Yards & warehouses

Trucks blocking gates, idle forklifts, drone-view yard state — the same brain works from any vantage point.

05 · CARE

Falls & wellbeing

"He fell at 14:02 and hasn't moved since" — detected in seconds, with the moment and the history attached.

06 · YOURS

Anything with frames

Rules are plain English and there's no training step — if you can describe what you care about, GAVI can watch for it.

How it works, technically

Every ~2 seconds, GAVI runs one perception cycle over the freshest burst of frames. The cycle has four stages, and the division of labour between them is the whole design:

1 · Observe — eyes with no preconceptions

A vision-language model receives a short burst of consecutive frames — so it can see motion, not snapshots — and returns the entities it can actually see: label, discriminative description, current attributes, a bounding box in the model's native grounding format, and how each entity moved across the burst. It is deliberately given no memory. That isolation is what keeps perception honest.

2 · Match — identity is arithmetic, not vibes

Each observation is assigned to a known identity by a deterministic matcher that fuses two signals a human guard uses: appearance (a compact visual signature from the entity's crop, kept as a small gallery per identity) and motion continuity (predicted position from the last sighting — things don't teleport). The fusion is solved as an optimal assignment problem. Identical twins stay separate because position separates them; a returning visitor is recognized because appearance survives the gap. Wins by a slim margin get flagged, not asserted.

3 · Apply — a world model that can't be gaslit

The resolved observation updates the registry: attributes refresh, dwell time accumulates, movement trails extend, first-seen thumbnails are saved. Enter and exit events are derived in code by diffing presence sets between cycles — the language model is never asked "did anyone leave?", so those events can't be hallucinated. Everything persists to disk; restart the process and the world resumes, absent entities intact.

4 · Reason — the narrator

Only when something changed, a reasoning pass reads the resolved changes plus the world state — including per-identity dwell and movement stats — and writes the story: state changes, actions, behavior patterns ("circling the same aisle for 12 minutes"), and inferences about off-screen gaps, each with a confidence. It also evaluates your plain-English watch rules against the whole timeline, which is how "unattended for over an hour" can trigger from a single glance at the log.

And when you ask it something

Questions skip the pipeline entirely: the world state and event timeline are compact text, so "who has been here longest?" is answered from memory in one call — with times, and with observed facts distinguished from inferences.

Latency budget: a perception cycle lands every 1.5–3 seconds. That's deliberate — GAVI is a long-horizon understanding layer, not a millisecond tripwire. For sub-second, single-condition alerting, the same system exposes a stateless fast path that runs a pool of concurrent per-frame checks. Use both: the tripwire fires, the world model explains.
